Categories

The categories come from two well-known wellbeing frameworks: the six pillars of lifestyle medicine (eating well, moving, restorative sleep, managing stress, social connection, and avoiding risky substances) and the Five Ways to Wellbeing (connect, be active, take notice, keep learning, give). Each one is a small daily habit worth building.

  • Workout

    Regular movement supports mood, energy and sleep. Any activity you enjoy counts.

    Be active · Physical activity

  • Cooking

    Making your own meals makes eating well the easy choice.

    Nutrition

  • Break / rest

    Pauses, wind-downs and screen-free time help manage stress and protect sleep.

    Take notice · Sleep & stress

  • Learn

    Picking up something new keeps your mind active, and adult learning is linked with fewer symptoms of anxiety and depression.

    Keep learning

  • Connect

    A call, a meal together, or a kind message. Relationships are a core part of health in both frameworks.

    Connect · Give · Social connection

  • Cleaning

    People who describe their homes as cluttered tend to report more stress, so small daily resets help.

    Stress

  • Planning

    A few minutes of planning keeps your priorities in view.

    Take notice

  • Regular

    Everything else: work, errands, appointments. It's the default if you're not sure.

Workouts and learning count a little more toward a day's color, since they take the most effort.

Sources: American College of Lifestyle Medicine · New Economics Foundation · The Thrive Lab · Saxbe & Repetti, 2010

Water

Every body is different. The 8 drops are your daily target, and each drop can be whatever amount you need — a glass, a bottle, or a cup of tea.

No tasks on a day? Water becomes that day's goal: fill all 8 drops for full bloom.

Moods

Pick one each day. Your moods fill in month by month at the bottom of the Garden.

Making time

Sprint, then rest

25 minutes of work, 5 minutes off. Short scheduled breaks help you get more done.

Longer break every 90 min

After about three sprints, step away for 15 minutes.

Little and often

Short daily sessions stick better than one long cram.

Test yourself

Write what you remember first, then check your notes.

Move on breaks

Stand, stretch, refill your water.

Name the next step

"Load the data" is easier to start than "do homework."

Cook once, eat three times

One prep session covers busy weeknights.

Clean in 10-minute pieces

One small chore a day keeps the weekend free.

Finish early

Aim to submit a couple of hours before a deadline.

Plan tonight

Five minutes before bed: pick tomorrow's priorities.

Sources: PubMed 2023 · Work-Fit · Retrieval Practice · Evidence Based Education · HBR
