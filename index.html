Making time for everything

The schedule in this planner is built from these. Breaks are points, not slack — skipping them costs you color.

  1. Work in short sprints with a real break after each.Every study block here is 25 minutes, then 5 off. In one small experiment, people given a 5-minute break every 20 minutes got more done in two hours than when breaks were up to them — and they wandered off less on their own. PubMed, 2023
  2. Take a longer break about every hour and a half.After three sprints you get 15 minutes away from the screen. MIT's Robert Pozen suggests roughly a 15-minute pause every 75–90 minutes, since focus tends to fade on about that cycle. Work-Fit
  3. Spread studying across the week instead of one long night.The same hours split into small daily sessions are remembered far better than one big block — which is why there's a study sprint every day, not a Sunday marathon. Retrieval Practice
  4. Test yourself instead of rereading.Pulling ideas out of memory strengthens them more than highlighting or rereading does. Your one-page summaries and cheat sheets are written from memory first, then checked. Evidence Based Education
  5. Make breaks move you.Stand, walk, refill water. A review of 80+ studies found regular breaks help both well-being and performance, but most people skip them or take the wrong kind. Harvard Business Review
  6. Cut homework into named steps."Do HW 4" is hard to start. "Read every question," "load the data," "first model," "write up" each fit inside one sprint, so every sitting ends with something done.
  7. Cook once, eat three times.Sunday's 90-minute prep covers the weekday protein and sides, so weeknight cooking is a 10-minute snack, not an hour.
  8. Clean in ten-minute pieces.One small chore a day plus a nightly kitchen reset keeps Sunday's reset to 45 minutes.
  9. Submit early; use your drops on purpose.Homework is due 11 pm Pacific on Wednesdays, but the plan aims for 9 pm. Your two lowest homework grades are dropped — treat those as planned relief for a rough week, not something to spend by accident.
  10. Plan tomorrow tonight.Five minutes before bed: look at tomorrow's list, move anything that won't fit, and decide which sprint matters most.
